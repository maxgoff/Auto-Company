## 1. FILTER (b), APPLIED MECHANICALLY — IT PASSES. RULED, AND THIS IS PRECEDENT.

### 1a. Does account creation constitute a "meeting"? **NO. The researcher is right. Adopted.**

This becomes precedent, so I state the reasoning rather than the conclusion.

Filter (b) reads: *a checkout a stranger can complete **without a meeting**.* The word doing the
work is **meeting**, and a meeting means **a human gatekeeper with the power to decline you**.
That is what `Contact sales`, `Let's talk`, `Request a demo`, `Get in touch`, and
`Schedule a time` all denote, and it is why they kill a candidate: they mean the vendor has
decided that a person must qualify you before you may pay. This company has no salespeople and
no legal entity, so a market that gatekeeps is a market we cannot enter.

**An email-and-password form is not a gatekeeper. It cannot say no.** It is a turnstile, not a
door. Ruling: **account creation, OAuth sign-in, and "no credit card required" trials do not
fail filter (b).** A checkout sitting behind self-serve registration satisfies it.

Guard against the obvious abuse of this precedent, stated now so nobody has to argue it later:
**a "waitlist," "request access," "invite only," or "apply for an account" IS a gatekeeper and
DOES fail (b)**, because a human decides whether you get in. The researcher checked for exactly
those strings and found zero. That was the right check.

## 5. THE ORPHAN-BRANCH ARCHITECTURE, JUDGED ON ITS OWN MERITS (as requested)

He asked for this to be judged independently because it survives all three candidates dying. It
does survive them, and my verdict is: **technically sound, not novel, and not a business.**

**Technically sound — the objections you listed are real but survivable, and the incumbent's
README documents the costs:**

| Objection | Verdict |
|---|---|
| `GITHUB_TOKEN` on forked PRs | **Real, and it is the expensive one.** A `pull_request` from a fork gets a read-only token — cannot push a branch, cannot comment. The documented fix is a **second `workflow_run` workflow** plus artifact hand-off, carrying an explicit *"DO NOT run actions/checkout here, for security reasons"* warning citing GitHub Security Lab's pwn-requests research. Solvable; a genuine onboarding tax and a genuine footgun. |
| Protected branches | Largely a non-issue — an orphan branch sits outside typical protection. But org **rulesets** can block all pushes, and many orgs default `GITHUB_TOKEN` to read-only, requiring `contents: write`. |
| Repo bloat | Real but minor for text; the branch grows monotonically and never gets pruned. |
| History rewrite | Low risk on a dedicated orphan branch nobody rebases. |
| Users objecting to an Action pushing branches | Real friction, empirically survivable — 1,704 workflow files accepted it. |

**Not novel, and this is the finding that matters:** it is `py-cov-action`'s shipped design, four
years old, 1,704 files, actively maintained (§4 KILL 1).

**Not a business — the fatal flaw is commercial, and it is structural:**

The researcher's aggregation-theory reading was correct and he drew the **wrong inference from
his own premise.** He wrote: GitHub aggregated the demand side completely, therefore value
accrues to whoever holds durable state adjacent to the workflow, therefore let us hold that state
in an orphan branch.

The correct inference from that premise is: **the aggregator eventually takes the adjacent layer,
because taking the adjacent layer is what aggregators do.** Roadmap #1160/#1210/#1258 is GitHub
doing exactly that, on schedule, right now. The orphan branch does not put us next to the money.
It puts us **in the path of the platform's own expansion, holding state the customer owns, with
no contract and no ability to withhold.**

**Keep the technique. It is a legitimate free tool for a free tool. Never build a paid product on
it.**

## 6. THE BYPASS OBJECTION — not disqualifying in general; disqualifying for us. And it is a symptom, not the disease.

He is right that every offline-licensed dev tool has this problem and most survive it. JetBrains,
Sublime, and a hundred commercial linters all ship code the customer can patch in thirty seconds.
So the bare objection is **not disqualifying** — it is the normal condition of the category, and
I will not kill a candidate for it.

**Now invert it, which is where it gets interesting.** *Why* do those tools survive bypass? Not
because the check is strong. Because the buyer is **a company with procurement, an auditor, and
legal exposure** — an organization that will not knowingly run a cracked license because the
downside of being caught dwarfs the $10 saved. Bypass survives on the buyer's institutional
goodwill.

**And that buyer is precisely the buyer this company decided it cannot serve.** We chose the
GitHub-Actions-only rail specifically to avoid needing a sales motion, a legal entity, and a
procurement conversation. So we would be selling to the self-serve individual — the buyer with
*no* procurement, *no* auditor, and no reason not to fork — at **$5–10/month**, a price at which
nobody's goodwill is worth the invoice.

**Ruling: the bypass is not the fatal flaw. It is a symptom of the fatal flaw** — we are trying to
sell something whose enforcement requires the goodwill of a buyer we specifically chose because
they owe us none. He was right to raise it against himself.
